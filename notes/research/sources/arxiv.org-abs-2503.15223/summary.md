## What the source claims

Empirical study of whether patches that pass SWE-bench / SWE-bench Verified tests are
actually correct. Central claim: testing is non-exhaustive, so "plausible" patches pass
tests yet diverge from developer intent, inflating reported resolution rates.

Verbatim quotes from the abstract:

> "because testing is rarely exhaustive, a patch may pass the tests but nevertheless fail
> to match the developers' expectations."

> "Our findings reveal critical weaknesses in SWE-bench's patch validation mechanism, which
> causes 7.8% of all patches to count as correct while failing the developer-written test
> suite."

> "our novel automated technique reveals that even more (29.6%) plausible patches induce
> different behavior than the ground truth patches."

> "These behavioral differences are often due to similar, but divergent implementations
> (46.8%) and due to generated patches that adapt more behavior than the ground truth
> patches (27.3%)."

> "Our manual inspection shows that 28.6% of behaviorally divergent patches are certainly
> incorrect."

> "Combined, the different weaknesses lead to an inflation of reported resolution rates by
> 6.2 absolute percent points."

## Method / evidence type

- Empirical study of "plausible patches generated by three state-of-the-art issue-solving
  tools evaluated on SWE-bench Verified."
- Patches "extensively test[ed] and inspect[ed]" and compared "against human-written ground
  truth patches."
- Core technique: **PatchDiff**, "a novel technique ... for differential patch testing,
  which automatically exposes behavioral discrepancies between two patches."
- Includes manual inspection of behaviorally divergent patches.

## Scope, limitations, and gaps (as observable from the abstract)

- Benchmark-bound: SWE-bench Verified only; generalisation to other benchmarks/tools not
  addressed in captured text.
- Only "three" issue-solving tools studied; tools not named in the abstract.
- "Certainly incorrect" rate (28.6%) is derived from manual inspection — subject to human
  judgment; inter-rater reliability not stated in abstract.
- Full methodology (how PatchDiff exposes discrepancies, sample sizes, per-tool breakdowns)
  is in the full paper, **not captured here** — only the arXiv abstract page was fetched.
